# WP-17: Form DSL: one declaration, TUI and WEB realisers, CRUD through intentsvcs

## Objective

**Declare a form once and let three consumers render it, because the alternative is three homes for one truth.** The TUI (`intent edit`), the browser face `intentd` serves, and ST0064's SwiftUI menubar app all present and edit the same entities. Each one built independently grows its own idea of which properties a criterion has, and they drift in the direction every three-home defect drifts: silently, in the copy nobody is looking at.

**The DSL declares LAYOUT and WIDGET, never the FIELD SET.** Field identity comes from the entity schema face, so a declaration naming a property that does not exist is refused by name rather than rendered blank -- which is the whole reason this is a DSL and not a template. The widget vocabulary is closed and declared for the same reason: an unknown widget is refused, never skipped.

**Every write goes through `intentsvcs` and no realiser touches SQLite** (D06). The realisers are clients exactly as the CLI and `intentd` are, and under D56 the daemon resolves the declaration server-side and emits `{entity, title, fields: [{name, label, widget, value, editable}]}` -- so **no renderer knows what a criterion is**, which is what makes a SwiftUI consumer possible at all.

## What a strawman found that the design would not have

A runnable ratatui strawman was driven against real ST0056 data -- **154 criteria, 161 tests, 297 attachments on one entity** -- and it found defects that were invisible on a small thread. Criterion text reaches **59,061 characters with zero line breaks** (`ST0057 AC-08.5`), 91 rows exceed 1,000 characters, and the median is 224: **any widget assuming a criterion fits on a line is wrong on real data.** The alignment gutter must be computed from the row set, not constant -- a hardcoded one collided on the first real render. Clipping belongs at render time, never at build time; truncating into the model broke the detail pane, and stripping emphasis into the model broke the list.

**The mode machine is a declared table the controller reads, not a diagram of one.** Leavability and reachability are asserted at startup and checkable headlessly, without a terminal -- the same law as `no_state_can_be_entered_and_not_left` on the ratified entity machines, applied to the interface. **A capability that works and is unlabelled is indistinguishable from one that does not exist**: the strawman's way back from an embedded editor was wired and invisible, so every key tried was reasonable and none was the one. That episode is why the mode is always on screen and ESC always reaches nav.
