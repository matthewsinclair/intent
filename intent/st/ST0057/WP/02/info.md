# WP-02: .intentfiles: the manifest and its refusing grammar

## Objective

The committed manifest that declares which artefacts are realised as on-disk files, with a grammar that refuses rather than skips.

## Deliverables

- `.intentfiles` at the project root of `intent/`, committed.
- One flat list of artefact ids, with many writers and no regeneration (D57-2: hv replaced the two-region design on 2026-08-19, and D57-9 retires the `# BEGIN INTENT` / `# END INTENT` markers). The lifecycle verbs, `st hydrate` / `st dehydrate`, `organize --default` and a human all edit the list; `organize` realises it and never rewrites it.
- Parser + writer: `pin` and `unpin` change one line and leave every other line byte-for-byte.

## The grammar, and why it REFUSES

`<SIGIL>:<ID>`, sigil in `STEELTHREAD | ISSUE`, optional trailing comment, nothing else. **A line the parser cannot read aborts the run with its line number.**

A skipped line drops an artefact from realisation and leaves an estate **indistinguishable from one that never listed it** -- the silent-drop shape v2.19.0 already paid for twice, in `ac gate`'s F1 fix and in the AT row grammar's `at lint`.

## Why two regions (retired 2026-08-19)

The first design split the file. hv replaced it with one flat list that nothing regenerates (D57-2), which leaves a pin nothing to be protected from. The collision it answered: a computed default and a written file collide. Without the split: pin ST0011 to read it, it closes, `organize` regenerates the block from status, **and the pin is gone along with the files, with nothing in the output naming the decision.**

## Why committed

The set it governs is committed. A machine-local manifest would let `organize` on one machine delete files another machine depends on, and land the deletion in a commit with no declared reason. Committed, hydration and dehydration are reviewable -- the diff shows the manifest line changing beside the files appearing or disappearing.
