`rust.yml` selects with a `paths:` ALLOW-LIST, which is the form `tests.yml`'s own comment names as this project's recurring false-green class.

**Measured 2026-09-22 by vc, surfaced by devbin-vc's 0110 sweep touching the neighbouring file.** `.github/workflows/tests.yml:6-10` argues, at length and deliberately, for `paths-ignore` over `paths:`:

> IGNORE-LIST, DELIBERATELY, NOT AN ALLOW-LIST. `paths-ignore` fails safe: forget an entry and the suite still runs. `paths:` fails SILENT -- forget an entry and the suite stops running while reporting nothing, which is the false-green class this project keeps closing. Only ever add here what has been measured to be inert.

`.github/workflows/rust.yml` uses `paths:` on BOTH triggers, listing `native/rust/**`, `schema/**`, `surface/**` and `.github/workflows/rust.yml`.

**So the two files hold opposite positions on the same question, and the one arguing against allow-lists is not the one that uses one.** The consequence is exactly what the comment predicts: a Rust-relevant path added outside those four globs stops being covered by the rust workflow, and nothing reports it -- the workflow simply does not run, which is indistinguishable from a workflow that ran and passed.

**WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED.** No such path is known to exist today; this is the shape of the exposure and not a live gap. The four globs may well cover everything the rust workflow needs, and nobody has enumerated what a change outside them would look like. The finding is that the SELECTION MODE contradicts the project's own stated reasoning in the file next door, not that coverage is currently missing.

**Why it is worth recording rather than fixing on sight.** Converting `paths:` to `paths-ignore` on `rust.yml` inverts what the workflow runs on and would make it run on every push, which is a real cost decision rather than a cleanup -- the rust workflow is the expensive one. The alternatives are to convert it, to leave it with the reasoning written down, or to enumerate what the allow-list omits and judge that. That is a decision for whoever owns Intent's CI cost, not a defect to patch.
