Measured 2026-09-13 on the delivered daemon at e70c3528a (pid 31992, started about 13:58:30Z by `intent daemon restart`), first noticed by dc at 14:07:09Z from `ps` while waiting on a load gate.

What was measured:

- `ps` at 14:07:09Z: 100% CPU, 1:53 CPU time over 8:39 elapsed. Later reads: 2:13 at 14:09:00Z, 2:18 five seconds later, 3:08 at 14:10:16Z, 3:48 at about 14:11:30Z. So the duty cycle was not one flat loop: stretches at 100% and stretches nearer 20 to 70%.
- `top` at about 14:11:30Z: SYSBSD 121,627,650 BSD syscalls cumulative, 113,898 context switches, SYSMACH 18,633. Against 3:48 of CPU that is on the order of a million syscalls a second while hot.
- `ps -M`: one thread carried it all, 2:54 system and 1:23 user at 97.9%, state R; every other thread under a second. By thread order that is the eighteenth thread, which in the `sample` listing is the store's serve thread spawned from `intentd::store::ProjectHandle::open`, not a tokio worker.
- `sample 31992 3`: 2341 samples, every leaf a wait (`__psynch_cvwait` 39795, `mach_msg2_trap` 4791, `__semwait_signal` 4567, `__workq_kernreturn` 2341, `kevent` 2341). The store thread sat 2339 of 2341 in `tokio::runtime::park::Inner::park` under `parking_lot::Condvar::wait_until_internal` reached through `pthread_cond_wait` (no timeout), and 2 samples in `intentd::store::serve` answering `Op::Graphql` for the app's `{threads{id}}` poll. A wait with no timeout that costs a core is returning at once every time: either an unparker signalling a million times a second from a thread that shows no CPU, or the wait returning spuriously.
- The store's `event_log` did not grow across the window, no file under the project tree changed in the two minutes before the read, no client was connected on the web port, the only Unix-socket client was the menubar app's five-second poll, and every git worktree lives under `/private/tmp`, outside the watched tree.
- `intent daemon restart` at about 14:13Z stopped it cleanly ("asked over the wire"). The replacement (pid 58837) read 0.0% at 8s and stayed at 0.0% for the next five minutes (5.9s of CPU in total, the post-start index pass included). So the spin is triggered, not built in.

What is known about the trigger: nothing that survives the evidence. From the CPU time, the onset was about 14:05Z, give or take the duty cycle. In that minute: dc's `cargo build -p intentd` in a detached worktree under an isolated HOME (no path to this daemon); the app polling `intent version`, `daemon status --format json` and `graphql {threads{id}}` every five seconds, as it had since 13:58Z; no commit (the last before the onset was 14:02:51Z, the next 14:13:56Z). The daemon log has no timestamps (0321), so the daemon's own account of the window is two lines.

What is NOT in question: the daemon source is byte-identical between 8a48430ee, the pair dc rehearsed green four times, and e70c3528a; the only library change between them is the `wb show` and `wb pickup` read shape (0322). So this is not a regression of the two items that landed before the tag. It may be present in 3.0.1 in the field, unmeasured: this host's load floor has never been attributed process by process.

Evidence kept in vc's scratchpad for this session: `intentd-31992-sample.txt`, `intentd-31992-evidence.txt` (the `ps -M` and `top` reads), `intentd-58837-cpu-trace.txt`. A watch is armed on the live daemon's CPU; on the next onset the capture is `sample` for five seconds, `lsof` for clients, and an lldb thread list with backtraces, so the hot thread is named by id rather than by order.
