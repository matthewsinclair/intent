Between an intentd restart and its first read of the account's usage endpoint, the estate page's plan usage box prints "usage endpoint failed: the account's usage endpoint is read by intentd's census tick, and this reading was not taken through it." Nothing failed. intentd has not read the endpoint yet, and that sentence describes a different case.

Seen on hv's live page at 11:53Z on 2026-10-27, after the restart onto 7efc01183c27; seconds later the estate op held a good reading (read_at 11:53:00.564Z, error null). After the restart onto f06241f83b56 (intentd pid 6781 started at 12:15:56Z by date -u), the first reading was read_at 12:17:10.804Z, so the wrong wording can stand for over a minute after a restart. ic found it; hv is the reporter.

Where it comes from. `estate::status_with` (intentsvcs/src/estate.rs) passes intentd's held endpoint reading to `usage::resolve` (intentsvcs/src/estate/usage.rs), and that reading is `None` until intentd's census tick first reads the endpoint. `resolve` maps `None` to `NO_ENDPOINT`, whose text and doc comment are written for a reader that is not intentd. The page (intentd/src/shell.html, the plan usage note) prints any `usage.error` after "usage endpoint failed: ". The text predates 0658: usage.rs carries it at ddb749ca7.

A shape for the ruling, not built: intentd before its first endpoint read reports that the reading is not taken yet, as a value distinct from a failure, and the page words it that way (eg "usage endpoint not read yet; the statusline fallback stands in"). A reader that is not intentd keeps `NO_ENDPOINT` as it is. The arms would cover intentd before its first read, after a good read, and after a failed read, and the CLI reader.
