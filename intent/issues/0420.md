## What happened

During dc's close run after migrate's landing (292414661, closes committed at 61a88ae21), 2026-09-16 between 16:46Z and 16:51Z, four of the run's store writes failed with `database is locked`: `issues edit 0407` twice (16:47:47Z and the re-issue at 16:48:00Z), `issues edit 0408` at 16:49:49Z, and `issues edit 0409` at 16:50:41Z. The re-issues for 0408 and 0409 succeeded. Each write was one command, with a settle read (`intent issues show`) in its own command before it.

The store's busy timeout is 5000 ms (`Store::BUSY_TIMEOUT_MS`, store.rs), so each failure was a write lock held for more than 5 s by another connection.

## What was measured

- `lsof` on `intent/.cache/intent.db` and `intent.db-wal` named only intentd 25378 (the delivered pair), at 0.0% CPU, with no other `intent` process. A short-lived process that took and released the lock before `lsof` ran would not appear there.
- The locks came about 5 s after the preceding settle read and 12 to 20 s after the preceding close. They did not come a few seconds after a write, so waiting for intentd's ingest to settle was not what freed them.
- `event_log` from 16:46:30Z shows only dc's own issue writes. The delivered pair predates the landed logging of whiteboard writes (vc decision 21 (2)), so a whiteboard or session-hook write in that window would not be logged.
- At vc's probe at 16:48:46Z, a `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` with a zero timeout took the lock at once and rolled back.
- `intent.db` was 152920064 bytes and `intent.db-wal` 98175512 bytes, the same size at both captures. That is a lead, not a cause.
- intentd's 3 s `sample`, taken about 5 s after each lock, holds no sqlite frames. The holder had already let go, so the capture proves nothing about intentd's own role.

Captures are in dc's scratchpad as `lock-0408a-{ls,lsof,sample}.txt` and `lock-0409a-{ls,lsof,sample}.txt`. They are lost at the next reboot.

## Not yet known

- Which connection holds the write lock. The candidates not yet excluded: intentd's own write path (an ingest or a checkpoint), short-lived `intent` processes from other sessions (the session hooks run on every prompt in every open Claude session), and the settle read itself.
