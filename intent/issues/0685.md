Found in the 3.3.0 estate sweep (ST0092 WP-11), 2026-11-11. In five boards of the sweep, Devbin and Webber among them, a commit of exactly what `intent wb init --apply` printed left the schema line of other boards dirty, and a second mechanical commit was needed.

**THE CAUSE IS NOT THE APPLY: IT NAMES EVERYTHING IT WRITES.** Measured by cc on 2026-11-12 with brew 3.3.0, on a scratch clone of Devbin at the parent of its sweep commit e10e51ee. The first board write a newer build makes renders every board from the store, so every board still at an older shape is re-rendered to the new one. An ordinary verb names each board that is not its runner's as `re-rendered: <path> (<owner>'s)`, and in the drive `intent sync --to-store` named cc's, dc's and hv's boards that way. Their owners never wrote them and never commit them. Every later verb, `wb init --apply` included, then finds those boards already holding its render, writes nothing to them and names nothing. Devbin's history shows the same thing: vc's board went to `intent/board@4.1` in aefbfcdf, while cc's, dc's and hv's stayed at 4.0 in git until the follow-up e197dea9. Webber's ic and vc boards were at 4.1 before its apply, and every other board was at 4.0.

**SO THE GAP IS THE WHITEBOARD PROTOCOL'S NINTH INVARIANT.** The re-render a new board shape forces on every board is one mechanical commit by the coordinating node or hv. The roster apply says it is that commit ("note: this is the roster's own mechanical commit"), but it cannot see a re-render an earlier verb left on disk.

**THE FIX, AS RULED** (intent-vc, 2026-11-12, under hv's pen, on cc's shape): `intent wb init --apply` also names, on `wrote:` lines, each `board.json` whose difference from HEAD is its schema line alone, read from git. A git read that fails is a note, never a silent skip. No helper is shared with the other roster acts until a second one needs it. Out of scope: the `(<owner>'s)` label an ordinary verb gives a shape-only re-render. cc's reading, open to vc: the dry run's `would write:` list is unchanged, because those files are already written and the apply names them only for the commit.
