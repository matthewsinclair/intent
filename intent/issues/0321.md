title: intentd.log lines carry no timestamp, so a restart's cause cannot be tied to a time
status: OPEN
severity: low
created: 2026-09-13
reporter: Matthew Sinclair
---

Measured 2026-09-13 during the post-fold process sweep. The running intentd (pid 14270) had started at 13:18Z, later than the daemon the previous sweep recorded, and the sweep asked why.

`~/.local/share/intent/intentd.log` answers with alternating `intentd listening on ...` and `intentd stopping: asked over the wire` lines, and no line carries a time. The only clock available is the file's mtime, which names the last write and nothing before it. Which stop was 13:18Z's, and what asked for it, is not answerable from the log. The behaviour itself was fine: a clean stop, a fresh listen, the delivered stamp (8a48430ee) on the new process.

Remedy: stamp every log line with a UTC time read from the clock at write time, and on a stop asked over the wire name the asker where the wire carries an identity (the CLI verb, the app, a peer's `daemon restart`). Severity low: the daemon behaved; the gap is in the record, and it costs a sweep the ability to distinguish a deliberate restart from a crash-and-respawn by anything other than the wording of one line.
