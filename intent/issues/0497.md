`intent wb migrate` writes the carry's own instant into the carried node's `heartbeat_at`. So a board nobody has touched for weeks renders as live at the moment of its carry, and nothing in the migrate report says the stamp moved.

**Measured on Riffle, 2026-09-21 (ic's carry, Riffle 02d1a8e, on the 510409ccc pair).** cc's hand-authored board was `status: paused` with `heartbeat_at: 2026-08-11T11:06Z`. After `intent wb migrate cc`, the rendered board reads `heartbeat_at: 2026-09-21 07:33Z`, still `status: paused`, and `intent wb show cc` gives `heartbeat 2026-09-21T07:33:50.693Z`. cc did nothing that morning; the carrier did. The migrate report's 41 lines carry every item and snapshot and say nothing about the header's stamp. Its only hint is the pre-migration copy, which still shows `2026-08-11T11:06Z`.

**Why it matters.** A carry is the carrier's act, not the node's. The heartbeat is what peers read for liveness, and it is what the 7-day reclaim rule counts from. So a carry resets the reclaim clock of a node nobody is running. On estates with an hv node (Baize, Molt, Prolix) the same carry would put a fresh stamp on hv's board, whose heartbeat is advisory but is still read.

**THE RESTAMP IS RULED, NOT ACCIDENTAL, AND THE FIX HAS TO LIVE INSIDE THAT RULING.** `Store::carry_header` (`native/rust/crates/intentsvcs/src/store.rs`, the doc above it) records it. The service re-reads `heartbeat_at` from the clock at the write, and the board's own claim goes to `authored_at` verbatim. That resolves ST0069 AC-14.4 (no function takes a timestamp; every stamp is the store's, in the write) against AC-14.9 (nothing dropped silently). The doc's reason: *what a board CLAIMED about its own liveness is the value we know may be invented.* **So the obvious remedy, carrying the header's `heartbeat_at` as read into `heartbeat_at`, reopens the hole AC-14.4 closes**, and is not the ask. The board's claim is already kept, in `authored_at`. What is missing is that nothing a reader sees says which of the two stamps a migrated node's liveness rests on.

**The ask, mechanism left to the plan:** `wb migrate`'s report names the restamp for each node it carries, giving both values, eg `heartbeat: 2026-08-11T11:06Z as authored, 2026-09-21T07:33Z at carry`. Possibly also: until a migrated node's own first write, the board view and `wb status` show its heartbeat as the carry's rather than as the node's, and reclaim counts from `authored_at`. The report line is the floor; the second half is the plan's call and may prove not worth its surface.

Severity low: no data is lost, since the claim survives in `authored_at` and in `.history/pre-migration/wip.md`. The harm is a misleading liveness reading on carried boards and a reclaim clock reset on nodes nobody runs. Size XS for the report line alone.



**CLOSED ON THE LANDING AT fb5a9b0d6 (ic, 2026-09-21), patch-id 7a86cae49, identical to the rebased bank vc judged from refs/bank/ic/0497-patch-2.** hv ruled it into the 3.2.0 cut.

**The report line, as landed:** `heartbeat: <authored> as authored, <carry> at carry`, printed once per carry just before the `by section` line. On the Riffle measurement above it would read `heartbeat: 2026-08-11T11:06Z as authored, 2026-09-21T07:33:50.693Z at carry`. Both values are read back from the row the carry wrote, through `Store::wb_node_heartbeats`, one SELECT of `heartbeat_at` and `authored_at`. **No clock is read on the side, so `Store::carry_header`'s ruling stands untouched**: the store stamps the heartbeat at the write and keeps the header's claim in `authored_at`, which resolves AC-14.4 against AC-14.9 (D42). A header that claimed no heartbeat reads `none as authored`, because every carry restamps and the line says so every time.

**The test** is `intent-cli/tests/wb_migrate_reports_the_restamp.rs`, one arm through the binary. It registers and migrates a board whose header claims `2026-08-11T11:06Z`. It asserts the authored value verbatim, and asserts the carry value equals the heartbeat `wb show` reads from the store, so no clock is predicted, and that the two differ. A peer with no header stamp is the negative control and reads `none as authored`. A mutation printing the carry stamp in both slots turned it red.
